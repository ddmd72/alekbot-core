# Decision: expiry is decided from content and enforced by a daily zero-LLM janitor, log-only until 2026-10-27, writing since (Facts S3, 2026-10-23)

**Decision.** Spec: `docs/10_rfcs/FACT_LIFECYCLE_RFC.md` D1, D2, D4, D5, D10, "S3 as built", "S4 as built"; owner's map: `docs/05_building_blocks/memory/README.md` §2.7, §5.5.
- The model decides each fact's end from its content (`date`, `horizon` or `none`, a required `expiry` argument); code computes `expiry_kind`, `ttl_days` and `expires_at` from it, in the owner's timezone, and refuses a decision that is born due. A cleanup pass (Stage 2a/2b, backstop, cluster) floors a due horizon to now + 2 days instead; a closing state ignores `expiry`; directives, principles and mindset facts are pinned to `none`.
- Code owns `reported_date` per pass (clamped into the batch span on conversation and email passes, kept on cleanup passes, inherited through `derived_from` for a cleanup create) and every state change appends a `lifecycle_events` entry.
- The janitor runs daily at 09:30 Europe/Madrid: it moves every current fact past its `expires_at` to `stale` (in place, never archived or deleted, text never read), refreshes the baseline of every account it changed, and stores one snapshot per account per day. It skips a due fact with no `expiry_kind`. It is **log-only** (counts `would_stale`, writes nothing to facts) unless `FACT_LIFECYCLE_SWEEP_DRY_RUN` is an explicit off value; S5 switches it on, and only on the owner's word.
- The breaker stops an account's stales while it has stale facts and no successful review within the review interval + 72 h, or when the pool is over 50. The **pool** is the stale facts plus the facts of open review questions (S4: a fact waiting for the owner is current, and the janitor skips it). Per run, at most 50 minus the pool are staled, oldest expiry first; the rest is `deferred` (owner, S3-D10, counted on the pool since S4). A job run is not retried and takes no lease, but it takes a **run guard** (a small document, 5 minutes) so that two runs never overlap; a second one answers `busy`.
- A merge is never dated before its newest source (S3-D11). On a cleanup pass a past-date refusal has no discard ("rewrite it as what happened with `{kind: none}`, or give a future date if the plan still holds") and an unanswered one is an abandoned refusal (🚨) (S3-D12). A copied expiry view keeps its `expires_at` only while the date is ahead; a date that is today or past is refused as born due.
- From S4 the janitor also enqueues the account's review (log-only mode too) and raises the review's alerts; the rules are in `fact_review_and_owner_questions.md`.
- **No Firestore TTL policy on the facts collection, and none on the two lifecycle collections.** A TTL policy deletes documents whose field has passed; an `expires_at` here is a decision to stale a fact, so a policy would erase the owner's memory on the day it expired. A test walks the tree and fails on such a policy for these collections; the docs state the rule in words and never spell the command.
- Owner rule for S5 and S4: when unsure, keep the fact.
- **S5 classify** (the one-time expiry decision for the facts stored before the switch): a standalone owner-run script, not a pass of the consolidation agent. One structured call per batch of 25 through the live consolidation agent, with the taxonomy's `temporal_classes` and `expiry` blocks verbatim plus a reviewable instruction file kept outside the prompt snapshot. When unsure the fact gets `none`. A dry run writes a report the owner reads; apply then replays that plan exactly, under the change token recorded at the scan, and skips a fact changed since. The janitor stays log-only until the owner's word.

**Rejected.**
- Deleting or archiving an expired fact: a review decides what an expired fact is now (S4), and a wrong horizon must be reversible.
- A model-written `ttl_days` or `expires_at`: models count dates badly; the model now sends the kind and the date or days, and never sees `ttl_days`.
- Refusing a due horizon on cleanup passes: 467 of 563 live facts have a `reported_date` older than 90 days, so almost every Stage 2a rewrite would have been refused; the floor sends the fact to review soon instead.
- A janitor lease: per fact the `update_time` precondition makes a double write a conflict. Only the per-run cap could be exceeded by two overlapping runs, and the short run guard (a document taken in a transaction, no fencing of single writes) closes that.
- A Scheduler retry: a retry after the 180 s deadline could overlap a running sweep.
- Classify through the consolidation tool loop: its born-due rule refuses a past `date` and its cleanup floor invents an `expires_at` for a due horizon, and its `reported_date` rules move a field classify must leave alone; those are exactly the facts classify exists to find.
- The classify instruction as a prompt-snapshot token: `snapshot_pull --check` would report it as drift, and the next pull would delete it as a file gone from the store.

**Revise when** a second janitor writer appears, the run takes longer than the 5-minute guard, or the pool cap of 50 proves too low or high after the log-only period (writing since 2026-10-27).
